# Project Management Leadership Lab — General Handbook

**Lab:** Project Management Leadership Lab (PML)  
**Track:** 12 — Project Management  
**Learning Intent:** **LEAD**  
**Maturity Stage:** Advanced  
**Core Intention:** Coordinate people, timelines, risks and execution toward business outcomes.  
**Student Outcome:** Build leadership and delivery capability.

> **Vision without delivery is a daydream.** PML develops the capability to turn strategy, commitments and plans into governed execution and measurable outcomes.

---

# 1. Purpose

PML develops the ability to **lead complex initiatives from intent to outcome** by aligning people, scope, timelines, risks, dependencies, decisions and governance.

The delivery leadership loop is:

**Align → Plan → Mobilize → Execute → Monitor → Decide → Adapt → Deliver → Learn**

Project management is therefore more than scheduling tasks. It is the disciplined coordination of **people, decisions and execution toward a meaningful business outcome**.

---

# 2. PML Philosophy

1. **Lead is your intent** — coordinate people, timelines, risks and execution toward outcomes.
2. **Outcomes over activity** — measure progress by results, not busyness.
3. **Govern with clarity** — establish roles, decisions and governance early.
4. **Manage risk before it manages you** — anticipate and mitigate continuously.
5. **Serve your stakeholders** — communicate, align and remove blockers.
6. **Step into the Advanced level** — own complexity, pressure and accountability.
7. **Deliver across the 11 GICS sectors** — adapt delivery leadership to context.
8. **Leadership serves the mission** — disciplined delivery turns transformation into reality.

# 6. Project Charter

Every significant initiative should establish a clear charter covering:

- Business problem
- Business objective
- Expected outcomes
- Scope
- Non-scope
- Success metrics
- Key stakeholders
- Governance
- Delivery approach
- Major milestones
- Assumptions
- Constraints
- Dependencies
- Initial risks
- Decision rights
- High-level resource needs

> **Do not allow execution to begin with fundamental ambiguity about why the project exists and what it must deliver.**

---

# 7. Scope & Requirements Governance

Control scope through:

**Requirement → Acceptance Criteria → Delivery → Validation → Approval**

Distinguish:

- In scope
- Out of scope
- Assumption
- Dependency
- Change request
- Defect
- Enhancement
- Decision

When scope changes, evaluate:

**Impact → Cost → Timeline → Resources → Risk → Value → Approval**

---

# 8. Work Breakdown & Planning

Translate outcomes into executable work:

**Outcome → Deliverables → Work Packages → Activities → Owners → Dependencies → Milestones**

A useful plan makes visible:

- Critical activities
- Dependencies
- Milestones
- Ownership
- Constraints
- Decision points
- Quality gates
- Readiness gates

The plan is a management instrument, not merely a schedule.

---

# 9. RAID Management

Maintain a disciplined **RAID** model:

### Risks
Potential future events that could affect outcomes.

### Assumptions
Conditions currently treated as true.

### Issues
Problems already affecting delivery.

### Dependencies
External or internal conditions required for progress.

For every material item identify:

**Description → Impact → Owner → Action → Due Date → Status → Escalation**

---

# 10. Governance Architecture

Establish decision forums appropriate to project complexity.

Typical layers:

### Executive / Steering
Direction, major decisions, escalations and business outcomes.

### Program / Project Governance
Scope, schedule, risk, dependencies, financial and delivery status.

### Design / Architecture Authority
Solution decisions, standards, exceptions and technical risks.

### Workstream Governance
Detailed execution, blockers and dependencies.

### Delivery Team
Daily coordination and execution.

Governance should accelerate decisions, not create bureaucracy for its own sake.

---

# 11. Stakeholder Management

For each stakeholder understand:

**Role → Interest → Influence → Concern → Desired Outcome → Engagement**

Communicate according to stakeholder needs.

A communication plan should define:

- Audience
- Message
- Purpose
- Channel
- Owner
- Frequency
- Escalation path

> **Leadership is service: remove blockers, create clarity and enable people to succeed.**

---

# 12. Project Performance Management

Track meaningful indicators such as:

- Milestone status
- Deliverable completion
- Schedule variance
- Scope changes
- Open risks
- Critical issues
- Dependency health
- Defect trends
- Test readiness
- Decision aging
- Resource constraints
- Budget / effort where applicable
- Business-value realization

Avoid measuring activity merely because it is easy to count.

---

# 13. Risk Management

Use:

**Identify → Analyze → Prioritize → Respond → Monitor → Escalate**

Response strategies may include:

- Avoid
- Reduce
- Transfer
- Accept
- Exploit
- Enhance
- Share

Every significant risk should have:

**Owner + Response + Trigger + Review Date**

---

# 14. Issue & Escalation Management

When an issue emerges:

1. Confirm the issue.
2. Establish impact.
3. Identify owner.
4. Identify root cause or working hypothesis.
5. Define options.
6. Assess consequences.
7. Decide escalation level.
8. Agree action.
9. Track to closure.
10. Capture lessons learned.

Escalation should be **early, evidence-based and solution-oriented**.

---

# 15. Decision Management

Projects slow down when decisions become invisible.

Maintain a decision log containing:

- Decision required
- Context
- Options
- Recommendation
- Decision owner
- Due date
- Decision
- Rationale
- Impact
- Follow-up action

> **A project manager does not need to make every decision. They must ensure the right decision is made by the right person at the right time.**

---

# 16. Delivery Troubleshooting

### Project is behind schedule
Assess critical path, scope, dependencies, resources and blockers.

### Team is busy but outcomes are not moving
Reconnect activity to deliverables and business outcomes.

### Stakeholders disagree
Return to objectives, decision rights, evidence and business impact.

### Risks keep becoming issues
Review risk identification, ownership and mitigation quality.

### Scope keeps expanding
Strengthen change control and reconnect requests to project objectives.

### Decisions are delayed
Clarify decision ownership, deadline and escalation path.

### Delivery team is overloaded
Reassess priorities, dependencies, capacity and sequencing.

### Executive confidence is falling
Increase transparency, provide evidence and establish a credible recovery plan.

---

# 17. Change & Adoption Management

A delivered solution only creates value when adopted.

Consider:

- Stakeholder readiness
- Communication
- Training
- Business-process change
- Role changes
- Adoption metrics
- Support readiness
- Organizational impacts
- Resistance
- Leadership sponsorship

Use:

**Prepare → Communicate → Enable → Adopt → Measure → Reinforce**

# 18. Quality & Readiness Gates

Before major transitions validate:

### Scope readiness
Are agreed deliverables complete?

### Solution readiness
Is the solution technically and functionally ready?

### Data readiness
Is required data validated?

### Integration readiness
Are interfaces ready?

### Testing readiness
Are required test outcomes achieved?

### Business readiness
Are users and processes prepared?

### Operational readiness
Can support teams operate the solution?

### Governance readiness
Are approvals and decisions complete?

# 22. PML Rule Book — Operationalized

### Do
- Lead toward outcomes.
- Establish governance and roles early.
- Maintain visible scope.
- Anticipate risks.
- Remove blockers.
- Communicate consistently.
- Track meaningful progress.
- Make decisions visible.
- Adapt leadership to context.
- Own accountability.

### Don't
- Confuse activity with progress.
- Allow governance ambiguity.
- Wait for risks to become crises.
- Lead only through command.
- Avoid difficult decisions.
- Leave stakeholders guessing.
- Manage without reliable status.
- Allow indecision to drift.
- Apply identical methods to every sector.
- Treat project management as mere scheduling.
