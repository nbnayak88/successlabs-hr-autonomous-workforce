> **Track:** 02 — Strategy & Architecture

> **Core Intention:** Learn how enterprises design capabilities, operating models, and future-state architectures  
> **Student Outcome:** Develop an architecture-thinking mindset

---

## 1. Purpose of the Lab

The **Strategy & Architecture Lab (SAL)** is where the learner moves from understanding how an enterprise works to designing how it should work.

SAL develops the ability to connect:

**Strategy → Capabilities → Operating Model → Architecture → Roadmap → Business Value**

The objective is not to produce diagrams for their own sake. Architecture is used to make business intent explicit, create coherent choices, manage complexity, and shape a feasible future state.

---

## 2. SAL Philosophy

### Design, don't just describe

A professional who only documents the current state is observing.

An architect asks:

- What is the enterprise trying to achieve?
- Which capabilities are required?
- What operating model supports those capabilities?
- What should the future state look like?
- Which architecture decisions enable it?
- What constraints and trade-offs exist?
- How can the transition be delivered?

### Architecture follows business intent

Every significant design decision should trace back to a business driver.

Use the chain:

**Business Driver → Outcome → Capability → Architecture Requirement → Design Decision → Value**

# 3. The SAL Mastery Model

SAL uses the universal **BAISI PAHACHA™ 22-step professional mastery framework**.

The 22 Pahacha remain universal across all SuperLabs. SAL changes their contextual meaning, depth, activities, and evidence with a stronger emphasis on architecture, strategy, design, trade-offs, and future-state thinking.

The SAL progression is:

**Understand → Frame → Model → Design → Validate → Govern → Transform**

# 7. Architecture Decision Framework

For every significant architectural choice:

### 1. Context
What problem are we solving?

### 2. Drivers
Why does it matter?

### 3. Requirements
What must the architecture achieve?

### 4. Options
What viable alternatives exist?

### 5. Evaluation
How does each option perform against the relevant criteria?

### 6. Decision
Which architecture is being adopted and why?

### 7. Consequences
What benefits, risks, constraints and future implications follow?

### 8. Governance
How will the decision be maintained and revisited?

Use **Architecture Decision Records (ADRs)** to preserve architectural reasoning.

# 8. Capability Mapping Standard

A capability describes **what the enterprise must be able to do**, not the organizational structure or application that currently performs it.

For each capability assess:

- Business importance
- Current maturity
- Strategic relevance
- Pain points
- Technology enablement
- Investment need
- Future-state ambition

Avoid defining capabilities around individual software products.

# 11. Architecture Principles

SAL encourages architecture principles that can guide decisions across domains.

Examples:

- Business value first
- Capability over application
- Data as a strategic asset
- Open and connected ecosystem
- API-led integration
- Experience-led design
- Scalable by design
- Security and privacy by design
- Automate first where appropriate
- Standardize before customizing
- Design for change
- Govern through principles and guardrails

Principles are decision constraints, not decorative statements.

# 15. SAL Rule Book

1. **Design, don't just describe.**
2. **Think capabilities over functions.**
3. **Always sketch the future state.**
4. **Connect strategy to structure.**
5. **Embrace ambiguity as part of architecture.**
6. **Build operating-model fluency.**
7. **Design across the 11 GICS sectors.**
8. **Architect for the world, not just the organization.**

### Working principles

- Start with business intent.
- Separate capability from implementation.
- Make assumptions explicit.
- Show alternatives and trade-offs.
- Design for evolution.
- Validate architecture against quality attributes.
- Keep the transition path visible.
- Communicate at the right level of abstraction.
- Treat governance as an enabler, not bureaucracy.
- Never let technology choice replace architectural reasoning.

---

# 16. Do's & Don'ts

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Start with business outcomes | Start with a product or technology |
| Think in capabilities | Design around org charts alone |
| Create a future-state vision | Document only the current state |
| Trace decisions to strategy | Make architecture decisions without intent |
| Show alternatives and trade-offs | Present one design as self-evidently correct |
| Consider people, process and technology | Treat technology as the entire architecture |
| Design for change | Assume today's architecture is permanent |
| Use diagrams to communicate | Hide architecture in long documents |
| Validate quality attributes | Optimize only one dimension |
| Plan the transition | Treat target architecture as the finish line |

# 17. SAL Learning Journey

### Stage 1 — Frame
Understand strategy, business context and architecture drivers.

### Stage 2 — Model
Map capabilities, value streams and operating models.

### Stage 3 — Assess
Understand the current architecture, gaps, risks and constraints.

### Stage 4 — Design
Create coherent target-state architecture.

### Stage 5 — Decide
Evaluate options and document architectural decisions.

### Stage 6 — Transition
Build a realistic roadmap from current to target state.

### Stage 7 — Govern
Establish principles, standards, decision rights and guardrails.

### Stage 8 — Transform
Use architecture to shape future business and operating models.

# 19. SAL Success Criteria

A learner demonstrates foundational SAL capability when they can:

- Explain the relationship between strategy and architecture.
- Identify architecture drivers.
- Think in business capabilities.
- Map capabilities to business outcomes.
- Explain an operating model.
- Distinguish current-state and future-state architecture.
- Develop architecture principles.
- Create a coherent architecture blueprint.
- Compare architecture options.
- Explain trade-offs and consequences.
- Create an architecture roadmap.
- Communicate architecture to technical and business stakeholders.
- Connect architecture decisions to measurable business value.
