# Global Industry Intelligence Lab — General Handbook

**Lab:** Global Industry Intelligence Lab (GIL)  
**Track:** 18 — Industry  
**Learning Intent:** **CONTEXTUALIZE**  
**Maturity Stage:** Advanced  
**Core Intention:** Understand how industries influence processes, systems and architecture decisions.  
**Student Outcome:** Develop an industry-aware consulting mindset.

> **A system without industry context is a tool without a purpose.** GIL develops the ability to understand sector realities, connect technology to business context, analyze evidence and design solutions that genuinely fit the industry being served.

---

# 1. Purpose

GIL is the **industry-context and sector-intelligence layer** of the 20-Lab ecosystem.

It helps professionals move from:

**Technology → Business → Industry → Context → Insight → Advisory**

The objective is not merely to know industry terminology. It is to understand **why an industry operates differently and how those differences should influence processes, systems, architecture and transformation decisions**.

The GIL loop is:

**SCAN → UNDERSTAND → MAP → ANALYZE → CONTEXTUALIZE → SYNTHESIZE → ADVISE → ADAPT → VALIDATE → UPDATE**

---

# 2. GIL Philosophy

1. **Contextualize is your intent** — understand how industry reality shapes technology and architecture decisions.
2. **Study the sector's reality** — learn its customers, economics, pressures, regulations and operating model.
3. **Map business context** — connect technical choices to business realities.
4. **Analyze, don't assume** — ground thinking in industry evidence and scenarios.
5. **Speak the industry's language** — understand sector terminology, metrics and priorities.
6. **Operate at the Advanced level** — synthesize technology and industry into advisory insight.
7. **Master all 11 GICS sectors** — develop broad industry literacy while building deeper expertise where appropriate.
8. **Context serves the mission** — sector-aware design creates experiences that fit the world they serve.

# 6. Industry Analysis Framework

Use:

**Industry → Value Chain → Business Model → Capabilities → Processes → Data → Systems → Architecture → Outcomes**

For every technical recommendation ask:

> **“What does this mean specifically for this industry?”**

This question prevents generic consulting.

---

# 7. Industry Value Chain Mapping

Map:

### Inputs
Resources, suppliers, capital, data and workforce.

### Core Activities
The activities that create the primary product or service.

### Enabling Activities
Technology, finance, HR, procurement, legal and other supporting capabilities.

### Outputs
Products, services, customer outcomes and economic value.

### Ecosystem
Partners, regulators, platforms, suppliers and customers.

Then identify:

**Value → Friction → Risk → Opportunity → Transformation**

---

# 8. Business Context Mapping

For every major technology or architecture decision, document:

**Industry Driver → Business Need → Capability → Process → System → Data → Architecture → Outcome**

Example:

**Regulatory pressure → stronger workforce controls → compliance capability → controlled HR process → HR platform → workforce data → security architecture → reduced compliance exposure**

The example illustrates the method; actual sector evidence must be established for the specific context.

# 14. Industry Troubleshooting

### Generic solution does not fit the client
Return to the sector's operating reality and constraints.

### Stakeholders use unfamiliar terminology
Build an industry glossary before designing the solution.

### Technical recommendation has weak business support
Map it explicitly to industry drivers, capabilities and outcomes.

### Two sectors appear similar
Compare their economics, customers, regulation, processes and value chains rather than assuming equivalence.

### Industry data is contradictory
Identify source quality, time period, definitions and population before drawing conclusions.

### Consultant relies on assumptions
Return to evidence and validate with industry sources or practitioners.

### Technology works technically but creates operational friction
Assess the sector's workflow, customer impact, workforce and regulatory context.

### Industry is changing rapidly
Track leading indicators and establish a recurring intelligence-review cycle.

# 20. GIL Rule Book — Operationalized

### Do
- Study the sector's reality before advising.
- Map business context.
- Connect technology to business outcomes.
- Use industry evidence.
- Learn sector terminology and metrics.
- Analyze rather than assume.
- Use realistic industry scenarios.
- Understand regulatory and customer differences.
- Build literacy across all 11 GICS sectors.
- Adapt solutions to sector-specific pressures.
- Keep industry intelligence current.
- Think like a trusted advisor.

### Don't
- Give generic advice without industry context.
- Treat technology as separate from business reality.
- Assume sectors operate identically.
- Rely on unsupported industry assumptions.
- Ignore regulation or customer expectations.
- Use outdated industry information without qualification.
- Confuse one company's practices with an entire industry.
- Apply one-size-fits-all architecture.
- Overstate uncertain industry trends.
- Stay at the technical layer when business context can be understood.
