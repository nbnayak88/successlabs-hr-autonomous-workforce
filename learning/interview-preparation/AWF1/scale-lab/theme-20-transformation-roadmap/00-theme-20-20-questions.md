## HR-AWF1-B20-Q01 — From Current State to Transformation

### Interview Question
How would you transform a fragmented HCM landscape into a coherent target state?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The organization had multiple HR applications, inconsistent processes, duplicated data, and fragmented employee experiences.

**Task:** I needed to define a transformation path rather than simply replace applications.

**Action:** I assessed business capabilities, employee journeys, processes, data, applications, integrations, security, operating model, and pain points. I defined target-state principles and prioritized capabilities that created the strongest foundation for subsequent transformation.

**Result:** The organization gained a structured path from fragmented current state to connected HCM.

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example
Employee Central could become the HCM core for trusted workforce data and employee lifecycle processes while surrounding capabilities are progressively connected.

### SME Probe
Why should transformation begin with capabilities and outcomes rather than applications?

## HR-AWF1-B20-Q02 — Target Operating Model

### Interview Question
How would you align an HCM target architecture with a target operating model?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The organization planned a new HCM platform but had not changed its fragmented HR operating model.

**Task:** I needed to ensure technology transformation was supported by organizational change.

**Action:** I mapped process ownership, decision rights, service responsibilities, data ownership, support model, governance, skills, and technology responsibilities to the target architecture.

**Result:** The organization moved toward an operating model capable of sustaining the new HCM environment.

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example
Employee Central ownership would be aligned across global process owners, HR operations, security, data, integrations, and application support.

### SME Probe
Why can a new HCM platform fail if the operating model remains unchanged?

## HR-AWF1-B20-Q04 — Roadmap Sequencing

### Interview Question
How do you decide what should be delivered first in an HCM transformation?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The transformation had many desirable capabilities but limited capacity.

**Task:** I needed to sequence the roadmap logically.

**Action:** I assessed business value, dependencies, readiness, risk, data maturity, integration prerequisites, organizational capacity, and time to value. I sequenced foundational capabilities before dependent outcomes.

**Result:** The roadmap became executable rather than a list of unrelated initiatives.

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example
Trusted employee data, core lifecycle processes, security, and integration foundations may precede advanced analytics and automation.

### SME Probe
Should the highest-value capability always be delivered first?

## HR-AWF1-B20-Q05 — Transformation Phasing

### Interview Question
When would you choose a phased HCM transformation instead of a big-bang implementation?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A global organization had high complexity across countries, integrations, data, and employee populations.

**Task:** I needed to select a transformation approach that balanced speed and risk.

**Action:** I assessed business criticality, readiness, data quality, integration dependencies, organizational change capacity, and deployment risk. I designed waves around logical business or capability boundaries.

**Result:** The organization could realize value progressively while reducing transformation risk.

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example
Employee Central could be deployed by region, employee population, or capability depending on dependencies and readiness.

### SME Probe
What makes a phased rollout architecturally coherent rather than fragmented?

### Interview Question

**Task:** I needed risk management to evolve with the roadmap.

## HR-AWF1-B20-Q19 — Transformation Course Correction

### Interview Question
What would you do if a transformation roadmap is no longer producing the expected business value?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Several roadmap initiatives were delivered, but expected business outcomes were not materializing.

**Task:** I needed to determine whether to continue, modify, or stop parts of the roadmap.

**Action:** I compared baseline and actual outcomes, examined adoption, process behavior, dependencies, assumptions, and investment, and identified where the transformation hypothesis had failed. I then re-prioritized based on evidence.

**Result:** The roadmap shifted toward initiatives with stronger measurable value rather than continuing because they were already planned.

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example
If Employee Central capabilities were live but manual work remained high, I would investigate process design, adoption, data quality, and integration rather than simply adding more functionality.

### SME Probe
What is the sunk-cost fallacy in transformation governance?

## HR-AWF1-B20-Q20 — Enterprise HCM Transformation

### Interview Question
How would you describe your approach to architecting a complete enterprise HCM transformation?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A global organization needed to move from fragmented HR technology and manual processes toward connected, intelligent, experience-led HCM.

**Task:** I needed to create an enterprise transformation journey rather than an application implementation plan.

**Action:** I started with business strategy and employee outcomes, assessed the current operating model and architecture, established a target HCM capability model, defined trusted workforce data, standardized priority processes, designed application and integration architecture, embedded security and governance, planned migration and adoption, and sequenced the transformation through measurable roadmap waves. I kept the architecture adaptable for analytics, automation, and AI.

**Result:** The organization gained a coherent transformation path from fragmented HR operations toward connected, data-driven, experience-led, and increasingly intelligent HCM.

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example
Employee Central can serve as the HCM core within an ecosystem spanning employee experience, recruiting, onboarding, learning, performance, compensation, succession, time, payroll, analytics, integration, identity, and future AI capabilities.

### SME Probe
What would you measure to prove that the organization has truly transformed rather than merely implemented Employee Central?

# Theme 20 Completion Standard

A learner completes **Theme 20 — Transformation & Roadmap** only when they can:

- Move from current-state assessment to target-state transformation.
- Align architecture with the target operating model.
- Define a measurable transformation North Star.
- Sequence capabilities using value, dependencies, readiness, and risk.
- Design phased global transformation without uncontrolled fragmentation.
- Treat data, integration, security, adoption, and AI readiness as roadmap dimensions.
- Manage multi-year transformation risk and funding.
- Govern and continuously refresh the roadmap.
- Course-correct based on realized business value.
- Demonstrate enterprise-level transformation architecture thinking.

**Quality rule:** Every scenario must demonstrate Situation → Task → Action → Result, include a distinct transformation/roadmap decision, provide an SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central example, and end with an SME Probe.
