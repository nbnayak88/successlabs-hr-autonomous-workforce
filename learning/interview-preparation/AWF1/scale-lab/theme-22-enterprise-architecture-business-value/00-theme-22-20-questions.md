## HR-AWF1-B22-Q01 — HCM as Enterprise Architecture Capability

### Interview Question
How would you position HCM within enterprise architecture rather than treating it as an HR application?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** An organization treated HR technology as a collection of departmental applications.

**Task:** I needed to establish HCM as an enterprise capability.

**Action:** I mapped workforce capabilities to business strategy, operating model, processes, data, applications, technology, integration, security, and experience. I connected workforce architecture to enterprise capabilities and strategic outcomes.

**Result:** HCM became part of enterprise architecture and business transformation rather than an isolated IT program.

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example
Employee Central can be positioned as an HCM core capability supporting workforce data and lifecycle processes within the broader enterprise ecosystem.

### SME Probe
What makes a capability an enterprise architecture concern?

## HR-AWF1-B22-Q05 — Architecture Trade-Offs

### Interview Question
How would you make an architecture trade-off between standardization and flexibility?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Business units wanted local HCM variations while enterprise leadership wanted a common architecture.

**Task:** I needed to balance global consistency and legitimate business needs.

**Action:** I classified variation by regulatory necessity, business value, employee impact, and preference. I standardized where the enterprise benefited and allowed controlled variation where justified.

**Result:** The architecture avoided both uncontrolled customization and harmful standardization.

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example
A global Employee Central template can provide common workforce structures while supporting justified country-specific requirements.

### SME Probe
What is the cost of excessive flexibility?

## HR-AWF1-B22-Q06 — Portfolio Rationalization

### Interview Question
How would you rationalize an enterprise HCM application portfolio?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Multiple HR applications provided overlapping capabilities.

**Task:** I needed to reduce complexity without losing critical business capability.

**Action:** I assessed capability coverage, business value, usage, technical health, integration complexity, cost, risk, strategic fit, and replacement readiness. I categorized applications for retain, modernize, replace, consolidate, or retire.

**Result:** The portfolio became simpler and more strategically aligned.

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example
Employee Central could consolidate selected core HR capabilities while legacy applications remain temporarily where justified by dependency or readiness.

### SME Probe
Why should application rationalization follow capability analysis?

# Theme 22 Completion Standard

- Position HCM as an enterprise capability.
- Map business capabilities to architecture and investment.
- Define and measure business value.
- Establish actionable architecture principles.
- Balance standardization, flexibility, and enterprise leverage.

- Architect trusted workforce data and connected ecosystems.
- Embed security, experience, resilience, and governance.
- Translate target architecture into transformation roadmaps.
- Measure architecture outcomes rather than documentation volume.
- Align technology investment with enterprise strategy.
- Demonstrate enterprise-architect-level business value thinking.
