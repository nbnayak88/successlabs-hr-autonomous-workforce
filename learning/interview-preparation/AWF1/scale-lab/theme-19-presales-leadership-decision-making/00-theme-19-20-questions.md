## HR-AWF1-B19-Q01 — Discovery-Led Presales

### Interview Question
How would you approach an HCM presales opportunity before presenting a solution?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A prospect requested a proposal for a new HCM platform.

**Task:** I needed to understand the business problem before positioning technology.

**Action:** I conducted discovery around business strategy, workforce challenges, employee journeys, operating model, current landscape, data, integrations, risks, and measurable outcomes. I then mapped those findings to solution capabilities.

**Result:** The proposal addressed the prospect's transformation needs rather than becoming a product feature presentation.

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example
I would position Employee Central around trusted workforce data, employee lifecycle, standardization, experience, and ecosystem connectivity rather than beginning with feature lists.

### SME Probe
What makes discovery-led presales different from product selling?

## HR-AWF1-B19-Q03 — Solution Demonstration

### Interview Question
How would you design an HCM solution demonstration for a senior client audience?

### SME Probe

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example

## HR-AWF1-B19-Q07 — Presales Architecture Trade-Off

### Interview Question
A prospect wants a highly customized HCM solution because it matches its legacy process. How would you handle the presales conversation?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The prospect expected the future platform to reproduce every legacy behavior.

**Task:** I needed to protect long-term solution quality while remaining responsive to the prospect.

**Action:** I separated mandatory business outcomes from legacy implementation choices. I demonstrated standard capability first, then evaluated justified extensions based on value, complexity, lifecycle cost, and maintainability.

**Result:** The prospect could see a path to modernization rather than simply recreating legacy technical debt.

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example
I would lead with Employee Central standard capabilities and introduce extensions only where a genuine business requirement remains.

### SME Probe
How can a presales architect avoid overpromising customization?

## HR-AWF1-B19-Q08 — Handling a Solution Gap

### Interview Question
What would you do if the proposed HCM platform cannot meet an important client requirement out of the box?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A critical requirement was not directly supported by standard capability.

**Task:** I needed to provide an honest but commercially useful response.

**Action:** I classified the gap as configuration, extension, integration, process change, roadmap dependency, or genuine product limitation. I evaluated alternatives and clearly communicated impact, cost, risk, and assumptions.

**Result:** The client received a credible solution path instead of an unrealistic promise.

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example
A requirement not supported directly in Employee Central could potentially be addressed through configuration, integration, controlled extension, process redesign, or another capability.

### SME Probe
When should a presales team explicitly say “not supported”?

## HR-AWF1-B19-Q12 — Difficult Commercial Trade-Off

### Interview Question
The client wants lower cost, faster delivery, and broader scope simultaneously. How would you respond?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A client expected maximum scope, minimum cost, and aggressive delivery timing.

**Task:** I needed to make the trade-off explicit.

**Action:** I showed the relationship between scope, effort, risk, timeline, quality, and resources. I proposed options such as phased delivery, reduced scope, or increased investment rather than pretending all objectives could be maximized simultaneously.

**Result:** The client selected an explicit and achievable delivery strategy.

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example
A phased Employee Central rollout could deliver core capabilities first while more complex integrations and localizations follow.

### SME Probe
How do you communicate the project “triangle” without sounding negative?

## HR-AWF1-B19-Q14 — Leadership Through Ambiguity

### Interview Question
How do you lead an HCM team when the target state is still evolving?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The organization had a strategic direction but several architecture decisions remained unresolved.

**Task:** I needed to maintain momentum without pretending all uncertainty was solved.

**Action:** I defined the stable principles and outcomes, identified decision dependencies, established short architecture cycles, and separated reversible decisions from high-impact commitments.

**Result:** Teams progressed while the architecture evolved deliberately.

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example
The Employee Central target could be established at capability level while detailed localization, integration, and extension decisions mature through architecture iterations.

### SME Probe

## HR-AWF1-B19-Q15 — Leadership Through Failure

### Interview Question
Describe how you would respond when an architecture decision you supported proves unsuccessful.

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A major design decision did not produce the expected outcome.

**Task:** I needed to protect the program and learn from the failure.

**Action:** I acknowledged the outcome, reviewed assumptions and evidence, identified what was known and unknown at decision time, defined corrective action, and captured reusable learning without assigning blame.

**Result:** The team recovered while improving its decision-making process.

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example
If an Employee Central design created unexpected process complexity, I would reassess the design against actual business behavior and redesign rather than defend the original choice.

### SME Probe
Why is accountability different from blame?

# Theme 19 Completion Standard

A learner completes **Theme 19 — Presales / Leadership / Decision Making** only when they can:

- Conduct discovery-led presales.
- Translate HCM capabilities into executive business value.
- Design scenario-based demonstrations.
- Position solutions objectively against decision criteria.
- Build credible RFP responses and estimates.
- Handle product gaps without overpromising.
- Protect architecture integrity under commercial pressure.
- Facilitate executive decisions and trade-offs.
- Lead architecture and cross-functional teams through influence.
- Build business cases and transformation roadmaps.
- Connect presales commitments to delivery reality.
- Demonstrate leadership, accountability, and trusted decision-making.

**Quality rule:** Every scenario must demonstrate Situation → Task → Action → Result, include a distinct presales/leadership/decision-making decision, provide an SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central example, and end with an SME Probe.
