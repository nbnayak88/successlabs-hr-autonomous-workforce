## HR-AWF1-B17-Q01 — Stakeholder Mapping

### Interview Question
How do you identify and prioritize stakeholders for a global HCM transformation?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A global HCM program involved HR, IT, business leaders, employees, payroll, security, and regional teams.

**Task:** I needed to understand who could influence, enable, block, or be affected by the transformation.

**Action:** I mapped stakeholders by decision authority, business impact, technical dependency, change impact, and level of interest. I defined engagement objectives and decision responsibilities for each group.

**Result:** The program had a deliberate stakeholder model rather than treating every stakeholder interaction identically.

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example
For Employee Central, I would distinguish global HR process owners, regional HR teams, IT architects, security, integration teams, managers, employees, and downstream application owners.

### SME Probe
How does stakeholder influence differ from stakeholder interest?

## HR-AWF1-B17-Q02 — Executive Alignment

### Interview Question
How would you gain executive alignment for an HCM architecture decision?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Executives supported the transformation but had different expectations about scope and outcomes.

**Task:** I needed to align the decision with business strategy.

**Action:** I framed the architecture decision around business outcomes, risk, cost, employee impact, dependencies, and strategic options rather than technical features. I presented the decision, alternatives, trade-offs, and recommendation concisely.

**Result:** Leadership could make a clear decision with a shared understanding of consequences.

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example
Rather than presenting Employee Central configuration choices, I would explain how the target HCM core supports workforce data, employee lifecycle, standardization, and downstream transformation.

### SME Probe
What information should an executive never have to extract from a 50-slide architecture deck?

## HR-AWF1-B17-Q03 — Conflicting Stakeholders

### Interview Question
How do you handle two senior stakeholders who strongly disagree about an HCM solution?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** One executive wanted standardization while another wanted extensive local flexibility.

**Task:** I needed to move the discussion from opinion to an evidence-based decision.

**Action:** I clarified the outcomes each stakeholder wanted, separated mandatory requirements from preferences, established decision criteria, quantified trade-offs, and presented viable options. I documented the final decision and its rationale.

**Result:** The disagreement became a structured architecture decision rather than a political contest.

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example
Global Employee Central template decisions can be evaluated against compliance, employee experience, operating cost, complexity, and long-term maintainability.

### SME Probe
What should an architect do if both stakeholders have equal decision authority?

## HR-AWF1-B17-Q04 — Regional Resistance

### Interview Question
A regional HR team strongly resists the global HCM template. How would you engage them?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A regional team believed the global design ignored important local needs.

**Task:** I needed to understand legitimate concerns while protecting global architecture.

**Action:** I conducted structured discovery, classified requirements into legal, operational, experience, and preference categories, and demonstrated which needs the global model could support. Where deviation was justified, I documented its scope and consequences.

**Result:** Resistance became a design conversation, and unnecessary localization was reduced.

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example
A global Employee Central template could accommodate justified country-specific fields, validations, and workflows without creating separate regional solutions.

### SME Probe
How do you avoid dismissing local resistance as simply “change resistance”?

## HR-AWF1-B17-Q05 — Business vs IT Language

### Interview Question
How do you explain a complex HCM architecture issue to a non-technical HR executive?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** An HR executive needed to approve an architecture decision involving data and integration complexity.

**Task:** I needed to make the decision understandable without oversimplifying the risk.

**Action:** I explained the issue through employee impact, process consequences, cost, risk, options, and business outcomes. I used technical terminology only where it improved decision quality.

**Result:** The executive understood the trade-off and could make an informed decision.

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example
Instead of explaining API mechanics first, I would explain how a source-of-truth decision affects employee data consistency across Employee Central and downstream systems.

### SME Probe
How do you know when technical detail is helping rather than distracting?

## HR-AWF1-B17-Q06 — Influencing Without Authority

### Interview Question
How do you influence a project team when you do not have direct authority over its members?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Architecture recommendations affected teams that reported to different leaders.

**Task:** I needed adoption without relying on organizational authority.

**Action:** I built credibility through evidence, clarified the problem, involved impacted teams early, made trade-offs transparent, and connected the recommendation to their objectives. I used governance only when consensus could not resolve a material decision.

**Result:** Teams adopted the architecture because they understood its value and implications.

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example
Integration, security, HR operations, and data teams could be aligned around a common Employee Central target architecture without a single team owning all decisions.

### SME Probe
What makes influence sustainable?

## HR-AWF1-B17-Q07 — Stakeholder Expectation Management

### Interview Question
How do you manage a stakeholder who expects delivery faster than the architecture allows?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A senior stakeholder expected a major HCM capability in a timeframe that created delivery and quality risk.

**Task:** I needed to protect the outcome without simply saying no.

**Action:** I explained the dependency chain, risks, minimum viable scope, and possible phased options. I showed what could be delivered safely by the requested date and what would require later releases.

**Result:** Expectations were reset around realistic commitments and measurable outcomes.

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example
Core employee data and critical lifecycle capabilities could be delivered first while complex integrations or localization follow in controlled waves.

### SME Probe
How do you challenge an unrealistic executive expectation professionally?

## HR-AWF1-B17-Q08 — Difficult Stakeholder

### Interview Question
How would you handle a stakeholder who repeatedly rejects architecture recommendations?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A stakeholder consistently rejected proposed HCM designs without providing clear reasons.

**Task:** I needed to uncover the underlying concern.

**Action:** I held a focused discussion to understand their business objectives, prior experiences, risks, and success criteria. I tested assumptions with evidence and invited them to define the decision criteria.

**Result:** The real concern became visible and the architecture discussion became constructive.

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example
A stakeholder rejecting Employee Central standardization may actually be concerned about compliance, employee experience, loss of local control, or operational accountability.

### SME Probe

## HR-AWF1-B17-Q09 — Decision Deadlock

### Interview Question
What do you do when stakeholders cannot reach an architecture decision?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Several teams remained deadlocked over an HCM design choice.

**Task:** I needed to prevent the decision from blocking delivery.

**Action:** I documented the decision statement, options, criteria, assumptions, risks, dependencies, and consequences. I identified the accountable decision owner and escalated only the unresolved business trade-off.

**Result:** The decision was made through governance rather than endless discussion.

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example
A decision between standard Employee Central capability and an extension could be resolved using business value, lifecycle cost, risk, and strategic architecture criteria.

### SME Probe
What should be escalated and what should an architect resolve independently?

## HR-AWF1-B17-Q11 — Requirements Negotiation

### Interview Question

**Result:** Priority became a shared decision rather than a competition for attention.

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example

## HR-AWF1-B17-Q14 — Escalation

### Interview Question
When should an architect escalate a stakeholder conflict?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A disagreement could not be resolved within the working team.

**Task:** I needed to determine whether escalation was necessary.

**Action:** I first attempted resolution using evidence, decision criteria, and accountable ownership. I escalated only when the conflict involved material business risk, policy, funding, strategic direction, or an authority boundary that the working team could not resolve.

**Result:** Escalation remained purposeful rather than becoming a substitute for stakeholder management.

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example
A dispute involving global policy, major compliance risk, or significant platform investment may require executive architecture governance.

### SME Probe
What evidence should accompany an escalation?

How would you tell a senior stakeholder that their preferred HCM solution is not viable?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A preferred solution created unacceptable security, cost, or architectural risk.

**Task:** I needed to challenge the preference without damaging trust.

**Action:** I acknowledged the intended business outcome, explained the evidence and constraints, showed the specific risks, and offered viable alternatives with trade-offs. I focused criticism on the solution, not the stakeholder.

**Result:** The stakeholder understood the recommendation and accepted an alternative path.

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example
If a proposed Employee Central customization created excessive technical debt, I would demonstrate the lifecycle consequences and offer a standard or controlled extension approach.

## HR-AWF1-B17-Q20 — Architect as Trusted Advisor

### Interview Question
What does excellent stakeholder management look like for an enterprise HCM architect?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A global HCM transformation required decisions across business strategy, employee experience, technology, data, security, and operations.

**Task:** I needed to act as more than a solution designer.

**Action:** I built relationships across executive, business, technical, regional, and user groups; translated between business and technology; surfaced hidden trade-offs; challenged assumptions respectfully; facilitated decisions; and kept architecture connected to measurable outcomes.

**Result:** Stakeholders involved architecture earlier, decisions became clearer, and the HCM transformation moved from technology implementation toward enterprise business change.

### SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central Example

# Theme 17 Completion Standard

A learner completes **Theme 17 — Stakeholder Management** only when they can:

- Map and prioritize stakeholders.
- Align executives around business outcomes and architecture decisions.
- Manage conflicting requirements and competing priorities.
- Influence without formal authority.
- Engage regional teams without dismissing legitimate local needs.
- Translate complex architecture into business language.
- Manage expectations and communicate difficult decisions.
- Build stakeholder trust and distributed change ownership.
- Run effective architecture governance and escalation.
- Create feedback loops without allowing uncontrolled scope expansion.
- Demonstrate trusted-advisor behavior across the HCM ecosystem.

**Quality rule:** Every scenario must demonstrate Situation → Task → Action → Result, include a distinct stakeholder-management decision, provide an SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central example, and end with an SME Probe.
